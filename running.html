How to do each type of session

Before every run: the 5 min walk

Walk briskly for 5 minutes before you start jogging. After a day at a desk your hips are stiff, your calves are cold and your feet haven't taken load for hours; the walk wakes all of that up gently.

Optional, last minute of the walk: 10 leg swings front-to-back and 10 side-to-side per leg (hold a wall or tree), then 8 slow walking lunges. These move your hips through their range without any holding or stretching, which helps the "less flexible" feeling.

Easy run

The backbone of the plan. Pace /km or slower, heart rate under 140. You should be able to talk in full sentences.

  • Your data says your "normal" runs average 147 bpm. That's too hard to be easy and too slow to make you faster. Slowing down here is the single biggest change.
  • Uphill, in heat, or late in the run your heart rate drifts. Slow down or walk 30 s; that's not failing.
  • Finish feeling you could do more.

Strides

4–6 × 20 s at the end of an easy run. Build smoothly to fast but relaxed (about 4:30/km, not a sprint), then walk back 40–60 s until fully recovered.

  • Grass or a track only: never tarmac or downhill (forefoot).
  • Land under your hips, not up on your toes. Tall posture, loose shoulders, quick light steps.
  • Skip them on any day the right forefoot feels sensitive.

Long run (Saturday)

Easy pace, heart rate under 140, flat route on soft surfaces where you can (paths, promenade). Capped at 80 minutes: plenty for a 10 km.

  • Check in with the right forefoot every 15 min. Discomfort building? Switch to 4 min run / 1 min walk, or cut it short.
  • Water on runs of 70 min or more.
  • "Last 2 km faster" runs: only if you feel good and the foot is quiet.

Fartlek

"Speed play": short brisk surges inside an easy run. After a 10 min easy warm-up, run e.g. 6 × 1 min brisk (7/10, around tempo pace /km or a touch quicker) with 1–2 min easy jog between.

Your first faster running in months: flat route, relaxed, no forcing. It replaces hill reps, which load the forefoot too much.

Tempo

Pace /km: comfortably hard, a few words at a time, HR ~155–165. Starts as 3 × 6 min and builds to 25 min continuous.

  • The most important session for a 10 km: it raises the speed you can hold without "tying up".
  • Even pace. If the last minutes are slower than the first, you started too fast; next time hold back 5 s/km.

Intervals (1 km reps)

After an 8 min warm-up + 3 strides: 3–6 × 1 km at /km with 90–120 s easy jog between (keep moving).

  • Hard, 8–9/10 by the end of each rep. All reps within 5 s of each other; the first must not be the fastest.
  • A track or flat path is kinder on the forefoot than pavement.
  • Form breaking down or forefoot complaining → stop there. It still counts.

Race pace

2–3 reps of 2–2.5 km at exactly 5:00/km. Check your watch every 200–300 m in the first km of each rep.

Your Runalyze data shows you start 40–90 s/km faster than you finish. These sessions train the feel of 5:00 so race day starts right. 5 s too fast is as wrong as 5 s too slow.

5 km time trial (weeks 6 and 12)

Rested legs, flat route, no stops. 15 min easy warm-up + 3 strides, then 5 km as fast as you can, evenly: first km no faster than target pace. Cool down 10 min.

Type the time into the paces box above. Your paces and predicted 10 km update straight away.

  • Warm up: 5 min walk, 10 min easy, 3 strides, done 10 min before the gun.
  • km 1–2: 5:00–5:05 (let people pass). km 3–7: 4:58–5:00. km 8–10: push if you can.

Rules that keep you healthy

Your right forefoot (metatarsalgia)

  • During a run: discomfort that reaches 3/10 or keeps getting worse → walk home. Make the next session easy.
  • Next morning: pain on the first steps out of bed → extra rest day. Two mornings in a row → see a physio.
  • Avoid: jumping, bounding, hill reps, fast downhills, strides on tarmac. These are what flared it before.
  • Shoes: cushioned, with a roomy toe box; don't lace tightly over the forefoot. Replace at roughly 600–800 km. Several pairs in your Runalyze log are well past that.

Recovery (slower than it used to be)

  • At most 2 hard sessions a week, always 48 h apart. Saturday long runs stay easy.
  • Every 3rd week is lighter (weeks 3, 6, 9, 12, 15). Don't "make up" the missing volume: the lighter week is where the fitness actually lands.
  • Morning readiness check before hard days: slept badly, resting HR 5+ above your usual ~43, or HRV well below your usual ~58 ms → swap for 30 min easy and do the hard session 1–2 days later.
  • Sleep is training: you average ~7 h; protect it in hard weeks.

Missed a session?

  • Never double up or cram two sessions into one day.
  • Missed a hard Tuesday → do it Wednesday if you feel fresh, and make Thursday easy.
  • Missed the Saturday long run → do a shorter version on Sunday if it's free; otherwise let it go.
  • Optional Sundays are never made up.
  • Ill? Above-the-neck cold: easy only. Fever or chest: rest, then 2 easy runs before anything hard.

Desk days and dark evenings

  • Get up every hour for 2 minutes. A set of calf raises at your desk counts towards the foot care.
  • 20:00 runs are dark from November: lights, something reflective, routes you know.
  • Late-slot sessions (21:15) are the five longest interval days. If that's too late, cut a rep rather than rush.

Foot care routine (Tue + Thu, 8 min, any time that day)

  1. Calf raises 2 × 15: up in 1 s, down in 3 s, on both feet. When that's easy, single-leg (hold a wall).
  2. Toe spreads + big-toe presses 2 × 10: spread all toes wide and hold 3 s; then press the big toe into the floor while lifting the others, and swap.
  3. Single-leg balance 2 × 30 s per side: barefoot if you can; harder version, eyes closed.

No weights, no gym, no stretching. This is what protects the forefoot when the running gets faster.
